## 2. Tell me about your strengths and weaknesses. Not necessarily technical.

One of my key strengths is **adaptability and process standardization**. Whenever a new process or technology needs to be introduced, I try to understand the requirement, establish a clear approach, and help the team adopt it consistently rather than creating additional complexity for other teams.

For example, in my current project, we were introducing security practices into existing CI/CD pipelines. Initially, some developers viewed security scans as blockers because they were concerned about pipeline delays and vulnerability findings. I worked with the relevant teams to improve onboarding, explain the purpose of the security checks, and make the process easier to follow. Over time, this helped improve adoption of the security practices.

Another strength is collaboration. I am comfortable coordinating with development, DevOps, and security teams to understand issues and work toward a common solution.

Regarding my weakness, when I encounter a technology or problem that I have not worked with before, I sometimes need additional time initially to understand it properly. However, I address this by breaking the problem into smaller parts, referring to official documentation, testing in a controlled environment, and applying what I learn practically.

I have been working on becoming faster at this initial learning stage while ensuring that I understand the solution rather than simply implementing it without sufficient clarity.

Interview tip: This weakness is credible because you also explain how you are improving it. Deliver it confidently rather than apologetically.

## 3. Tell me about the biggest challenge you faced in your existing project and how you addressed it.

Your transcript included both a compliance-evidence challenge and a developer-adoption challenge. The following answer combines them into one coherent example while keeping the focus on the more substantial DevSecOps challenge.

One of the biggest challenges in my current project was introducing and standardizing DevSecOps practices across existing application pipelines.

Our project supports payment gateway solutions, so security and compliance are important. Initially, the delivery process was more focused on traditional DevOps activities such as building and deploying applications. When we started introducing security checks into the CI/CD pipelines, some development teams viewed vulnerability findings and security gates as blockers to their regular delivery timelines.

The challenge was not just integrating the tools technically; it was also helping teams understand the purpose of the checks and adopt the new process consistently.

I worked with the relevant teams on security-tool onboarding, pipeline standardization, and explaining how scans identify vulnerabilities early in the development lifecycle. We also helped developers understand how to interpret findings, prioritize remediation, and follow the required onboarding and security processes.

In parallel, we identified gaps in providing consistent evidence for compliance audits. We worked on improving the process for tracking the required security activities and making the relevant evidence easier to provide during audits.

As a result, the teams became more familiar with the security workflow, adoption improved, and the process became more standardized. The main lesson I learned was that successful DevSecOps implementation requires not only the right tools and automation but also collaboration, clear processes, and developer awareness.

If the interviewer asks, “What exactly was your contribution?”

Explain your personal contribution specifically: security-tool onboarding, pipeline standardization, coordinating with developers, helping interpret scan findings, and improving evidence-tracking processes. Mention measurable reductions in remediation time or audit effort only if you have actual figures to support them.
